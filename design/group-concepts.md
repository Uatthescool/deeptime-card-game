# Group Concepts (on paper)

A Group card is a Concept named for a collective noun. It plays and counts as a Concept for every rule, including the Concept allowance, Resourceful and Dead End. It uses the Concept frame with a small "Concept · Group" label.

Purposes:
- Teach group names as a fun fact.
- Supply more card names.
- Bring in animals that would never be Creature cards.

Any deck can use them. Effects are argued from the group's real biology, like Creature Tags. Flavor text must be honest about where the term comes from: some date to 15th-century hunting lists (the Book of St Albans, 1486), and many are modern coinages.

## Effects in progress

Not in the game yet; the cards still show the placeholder text.

- **Parliament of Owls:** Draw 3 cards. Shuffle one of the cards drawn this way into your deck and send another of them to your History Pile.
- **Kaleidoscope of Butterflies:** Return a Creature on your side to your hand. You may play it again as an extra Creature play; if you do, your Development Phase ends once that play's effects have resolved. Graviportal Creatures can't be returned. Biology: metamorphosis.
- **Dazzle of Zebras:** Your opponent shuffles their hand into their deck, then draws 3 cards. Flavor must say stripes *may* confuse predators; the best-supported explanation is deterring biting flies.
- **Tower of Giraffes:** Search your deck for an Artiodactyl, add it to your hand, then shuffle your deck. If it has Overreach, you may play it into a Niche at once as an extra Creature play, following normal Level rules. No Artiodactyl has Overreach yet; the bonus waits for a high-browsing one, such as a giraffe relative. Artiodactyls include the whales, so this can fetch a Blue Whale.
- Congress of Baboons was dropped entirely.

All effects are undecided.

| Cauldron of Bats | Modern; "colony" is the usual term |
| Murder of Crows | Medieval; in use by about 1475 and in the 1486 Book of St Albans |
| Parliament of Owls | Echoes Chaucer's "Parlement of Foules" (all birds); applied to owls and popularized by C. S. Lewis's *The Silver Chair* (1953) |
| Tower of Giraffes | Modern (about mid-20th century); a "tower" when standing, a "journey" when moving |
| Dazzle of Zebras | Modern; "zeal" is also used |
| Conspiracy of Lemurs | Modern; no traceable origin |
| Flamboyance of Flamingos | Modern, about the 1970s |
| Crash of Rhinoceroses | Modern |
| Kaleidoscope of Butterflies | Modern; no traceable origin found |
| Murmuration of Starlings | Medieval; "a Murmuracion of stares" in the 1486 Book of St Albans. Murmurations are real flocking behaviour that physicists study |
| Skulk of Foxes | Medieval; in the 1486 Book of St Albans |
| Business of Ferrets | Medieval; "a Besynes of ferettis" in the 1486 Book of St Albans, probably meaning "busyness" |
