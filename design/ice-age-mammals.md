Epoch notes:
- Pakicetus is about 48.5 million years old and Ambulocetus about 48–47, both Eocene.
- Basilosaurus isis is late Eocene, 37.8–33.9 million years ago, which is still Middle under this convention.

- Hyaenodon horridus runs from about 37 to 30 million years ago, across the end of the Eocene into the Oligocene.
- Mastodon begins in the late Pliocene, but almost all its record is Pleistocene.

## Flavor text

**Pakicetus.** Named for Pakistan, where its fossils were found, Pakicetus was one of the earliest whales, yet it lived mostly on land, wading in shallow streams with its eyes set high on its skull like a crocodile's.

**Ambulocetus.** Its name means "walking whale that swims." Ambulocetus could still walk on land, swam with large hind feet, and likely hunted from ambush in shallow water like a crocodile.

**Basilosaurus.** Named "king lizard" by a discoverer who mistook it for a marine reptile, Basilosaurus was a whale that still kept tiny hind legs, far too small to walk on.

**Phiomia.** Named for Egypt's Fayum, home to many early elephant relatives, Phiomia had a short trunk and tusks in both jaws.

**Steppe Bison.** Painted by Ice Age artists on the walls of caves like Altamira and Lascaux, steppe bison roamed from Europe across Beringia into North America.

**Castoroides.** Castoroides was a beaver nearly the size of a black bear, but its bones show it ate water plants rather than trees, so it likely never felled trees or built dams.

**Glyptodon.** A giant armadillo, as ancient DNA confirms, Glyptodon wore a shell of more than a thousand bony plates fused into a single dome.

**Hyaenodon.** Actually neither hyena nor dog, the wolf-like Hyaenodon belonged to an extinct group of predators and had several pairs of scissor-like slicing teeth, where cats and dogs have only one.

**Hippopotamus.** Hippos are the closest living relatives of whales, and about 125,000 years ago, during a warm spell between ice ages, they wallowed in the Thames where London now stands.

**Mastodon.** A bone point lodged in a mastodon rib from Washington State suggests people hunted mastodons about 13,800 years ago; Thomas Jefferson later thought they might still roam the unexplored West, and in 1801 Charles Willson Peale mounted one dug up in New York as America's first displayed fossil skeleton.

**Woolly Rhino.** Painted in Chauvet Cave more than 30,000 years ago, woolly rhinos grew their front horns in yearly bands like tree rings, flattened side to side, possibly for sweeping snow off buried grass.

## Supports

15 of 18 chosen; 3 still to design. These are in the Deck 4 preview with the wording below; none of their effects are built yet.

Shared with other decks: Wildfire, Drought, Island Dwarfism, Cryptic Camouflage, Evolutionary Radiation, Whale Fall, Obligate Migration, Dig Site Excavation, Natural Selection, Rapid Speciation, Biogenesis, Genetic Drift. Geological Boundary is left out so it can't end Ice Age, and no other Continuous Event goes in, since one would push Ice Age out of the Event Zone.

**Ice Age** (Continuous Event). For overrides, Synapsids from the Paleogene, Neogene, or Quaternary match every Time Period. Creatures from the Cambrian, Triassic, Jurassic, or Cretaceous contribute 1 less Victory Level. When Ice Age leaves your Event Zone, your Creatures contribute no Victory Levels until the end of your third turn after it left.

**Paleocene–Eocene Thermal Maximum** (Instant Event). Send a Level 0 or 1 Creature on the board to its owner's History Pile. If it was yours, you may search your deck for up to two Creatures that share at least two taxa listed AFTER its Primary Taxon, reveal them, add them to your hand, then shuffle your deck. If it was your opponent's, send the Event in your Event Zone, if any, to your History Pile.
- "AFTER" is capitalized on purpose. Two shared taxa after Synapsid means a close relative: sending Pakicetus, Ambulocetus or Steppe Bison finds the artiodactyls; Phiomia the proboscideans; Dire Wolf the carnivorans; Glyptodon Megatherium. Castoroides, Hyaenodon, Lystrosaurus and Thrinaxodon find nothing.
- The opponent branch costing your own Event (Ice Age, in this deck) is intended, so it isn't free removal.

**Feedback Loops** (Concept). Search your deck for a card whose name appears below, reveal it, add it to your hand, then shuffle your deck. During your Development Phase, while Feedback Loops is in your History Pile, you may send a card whose name appears below from your hand to your History Pile. If you do, return Feedback Loops to your hand.
- Ice Age
- Paleocene–Eocene Thermal Maximum
- Drought
- Snowball Earth
- The Great Dying
- The Great Oxidation Event

The list is every card that is a feedback loop or the result of one, including future cards: Snowball Earth, The Great Dying and The Great Oxidation Event aren't cards yet, and their future cards must use exactly these names. Mainly it searches for Ice Age. The History Pile ability makes the card a literal feedback loop.
